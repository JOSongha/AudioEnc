# §4.3 PCA / Distance results

Generated by `analyze_pca_distance.py`. See `extract_embeddings.py` and `manifest.jsonl` for inputs.

## Dataset

- **Source**: IEMOCAP scripted plays (`script01_1`, `script02_2`, `script03_2`)
- **Speakers**: 10 (5 sessions x 2 genders)
- **Transcripts**: 248 (line-indices common to all 5 sessions per gender)
- **Total utterances per encoder**: 1240

Same `(script, line_idx)` across sessions = same transcript spoken by 5 different speakers (gender-matched).
VCTK / LibriTTS-R parallel-speaker subsets were not on the cluster; IEMOCAP scripted-line shared indices
across the 5 sessions provide a clean parallel-speaker substitute (same scripted dialogue line, different actor).

## Method recap

Per utterance we extract:
- **encoder**: raw audio encoder output (DAC-VAE latent / Whisper encoder hidden state)
- **projector**: AudioProjector output (in 2560-dim LM-embedding space)
- **lm_L{0,4,8,12,16,20,24,28}**: LM hidden states at sparse depth, captured via per-layer forward hooks
  (the custom `Qwen3_5AETextModel` does not propagate `output_hidden_states`, so we hook each decoder layer).

We feed the projector output as `inputs_embeds` flanked by the trained `<|audio_start|>` / `<|audio_end|>` token
embeddings, with no surrounding text prompt — this isolates the LM's processing of the audio signal itself.
Each utterance vector is mean-pooled across the audio frame range only.

The cosine-distance ratio is

```
ratio = mean_cos(audio_i, audio_j  | same transcript, different speaker)
      / mean_cos(audio_i, audio_j  | same speaker,    different transcript)
```

**Interpretation**: ratio > 1 ⇒ speaker information dominates over content (different speakers stay further apart than different content). ratio = 1 ⇒ speaker and transcript variation are matched. ratio < 1 ⇒ content dominates over speaker.

## Findings

1. **DAC-VAE preserves speaker info at the encoder stage.** Its raw encoder ratio is 0.976 — essentially balanced between speaker and content axes — consistent with a reconstruction-target codec that has to retain timbre, prosody, and pitch.
2. **Whisper encoders are speaker-invariant from the start.** Both Whisper-tiny (0.848) and Whisper-small (0.846) sit ~0.85 at the encoder, content already dominating — consistent with their ASR pretraining objective which actively discards speaker variability.
3. **Projector + LM monotonically suppress DAC's residual speaker info.** DAC drops by Δ=0.104 between encoder and LM L24, converging toward the Whisper-level plateau. Whisper variants are nearly flat across LM depth (Δ=-0.010 for tiny, Δ=-0.009 for small) since the LM has very little speaker information to attenuate to begin with.
4. **The projector is where the gap closes.** For DAC, the largest single-stage drop is encoder→projector (0.976 → 0.912). After the projector, DAC and Whisper sit on similar curves — i.e. once both encoders' outputs land in LM-embedding space, the LM treats them as semantically-aligned token-like vectors and only modestly modulates the speaker axis.
5. **Whisper-small ≈ Whisper-tiny under fixed LM.** Increasing Whisper capacity does not change the speaker/content tradeoff (both within and across distances scale together), implying the LM-side adaptation is the binding factor for §4.3-style probing.

## Caveats

- Mean-pooling over time collapses prosodic dynamics that may carry additional speaker signal; a frame-level probe could reveal more.
- IEMOCAP scripted-play renditions are emotionally varied even within the same line, so within-speaker distance is inflated by emotional variation, which biases the ratio downward across all encoders. The ranking across encoders is preserved, but absolute ratio values should not be over-interpreted.
- Cross-corpus generalization (e.g. VCTK rainbow passage / LibriTTS-R speaker subsets) was not run because those corpora are not on this cluster; if needed I can pull a small VCTK subset from datashare.ed.ac.uk.
